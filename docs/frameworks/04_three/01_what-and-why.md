# three.js, what it is and why this one

## What it is

WebGL rendering. Used for one view: the pile as a three-dimensional surface, with
the scalar field on it selectable and a hover readout giving true heights. MIT.

## Why this and not something else

The interactive-visualization rubric gates 3D behind a written justification naming
the third independent variable, and rejects it otherwise. Two views here pass that gate and the rest
do not.

The pile passes because height over a two-dimensional pad IS a third independent variable, and
because the question a reader has (where is the crest, where is the toe, which flank did this cut
come from) is a spatial question about a surface. The cutaway passes for the same reason. Every other
view in the product is a line chart or a heatmap, because that is what reads more precisely.

Vertical exaggeration is applied and PRINTED on the legend: a 55 m pile on a 192 m pad is invisible
at true scale, and silently stretching it without saying so would be a lie about the geometry.
